Phil-IRI in BASA

How the assessment works, what BASA changes, and why

For the BASA team. This explains the Philippine Informal Reading Inventory (Phil-IRI) as schools run it without BASA, then how BASA records and scores it. The last part answers the questions a panel is likely to ask, each with how it is done without BASA.

The rules come from three sources:

  • the Phil-IRI Manual 2018 (Grades 4–6);
  • the Phil-IRI for JHS Manual 2024 (Grades 7–10), Tables 6–8;
  • the reading coordinator's workbook, the Excel file a reading coordinator uses today to score Phil-IRI and plan ARAL. BASA was checked against it, and where the coordinator's practice differs from the manual, BASA follows the coordinator.

1. What Phil-IRI is

  • DepEd's classroom reading inventory. In BASA: English, Grades 4 to 10. CRLA covers Grades 1–3.
  • Group Screening Test (GST): a written comprehension test given to the whole class first. It has 20 items in Grades 4–6 (pass mark 14) and 40 items in Grades 7–10 (pass mark 28), split into literal, inferential and critical questions.
  • Individual reading assessment: each learner reads a graded passage aloud while the teacher marks the miscues and times the reading. The learner then answers questions about the passage.
  • Rounds: BOSY (pretest), MOSY #1–#3, EOSY (posttest).
  • For each reading, three measurements:
    • Word recognition: the % of words read correctly;
    • Reading speed: words per minute;
    • Comprehension: the % of answers correct.
  • They combine into a reading profile (Independent, Instructional, Frustration, Struggling, Non-Reader) and an adjusted reading level: the grade the learner actually reads at, e.g. a Grade 8 learner reading at Grade 5 is "3 levels down".
  • Phil-IRI results feed DepEd's NLA portal (Form 2b), the BOSY masterlist, the Summer Remediation Program, and ARAL-Reading placement in Grades 4–10.

2. Phil-IRI without BASA

  1. GST: the class answers on paper; the teacher checks and totals by hand.
  2. Individual reading:
    • the teacher picks a passage from the booklet;
    • the learner reads aloud while the teacher marks miscues on a photocopy and times with a stopwatch;
    • the learner answers the questions;
    • the teacher counts words and miscues and computes the % and wpm by hand or calculator, then looks the levels up in the manual's tables.
  3. Encoding: the coordinator types every score into a large workbook (one sheet per section, about 50 sheets per grade). Formulas give levels, adjusted levels and summary sheets.
  4. Tracking passages: the teacher must remember which passage each learner already read, so it is not repeated in a later round.
  5. Reporting: the coordinator reads totals off the workbook and types them into the NLA portal, then builds the BOSY masterlist, the SRP list and the ARAL lists from the same sheets.
Where it goes wrong without BASA
  • Counting errors in miscues, words, seconds and percentages, and wrong table look-ups.
  • Repeated passages. A learner given a passage they already read scores higher because they remember it.
  • Formula mistakes go unnoticed. A formula copied wrongly into one round changes every learner's result in that round, and nothing shows it.
  • Missing learners vanish. A learner never assessed simply isn't counted, which makes the school's numbers look better than they are.
  • Lost history. Results that change when a cut-off cell is edited; no record of which cut-offs a past report used.
  • Retyping. The same numbers are typed again into the NLA portal and several lists.

3. Phil-IRI in BASA, step by step

  1. GST. The tutor enters each learner's score: either the total, or the literal, inferential and critical parts. BASA shows who passed. A learner with only a GST at BOSY gets a starting grade from it, as a reference until the individual reading confirms it.
  2. Start a session for one learner and one round. BASA checks that the section is Grade 4–10 and the learner is actively enrolled.
  3. Pick a passage. BASA lists only passages of the learner's grade that the learner has never read before, in any round or year and in any collection. The same text in two collections counts as one passage. Passages labelled for the current round come first, but any grade-level passage may be used in any round.
  4. Choose how it is read:
    • whole text: timed;
    • one minute: the words read in a minute are the wpm;
    • silent: timed, no miscues, so word recognition counts as 100% and only speed and comprehension matter.
  5. Mark miscues by tapping words in the passage. There are seven types (mispronunciation, omission, substitution, insertion, repetition, transposition, reversal); all of them count. A self-correction is recorded but not counted.
  6. Answer the questions and score the rubric. Choice answers are checked against the key automatically. BASA shows the reading's result after each save.
  7. Complete the session. The tutor can reopen it to fix something. A session started by mistake can be discarded.
  8. Finalize the round (admin or coordinator). Completed sessions of that section and round can no longer change, and the cut-offs in force are stored with them.
  9. Import (admin app). The coordinator's existing workbook, or a filled BASA scoresheet, can be read into BASA. BASA shows a preview, and the import needs a confirmation code. Readings without a passage in BASA are stored as "Untitled", with their typed word count.

4. Scoring one reading

All percentages are rounded to a whole percent before the band is chosen, because the bands are whole numbers (96.5% → 97% → Independent). Every band below is a default that a region or school can change. The two combination tables (Table 8) are fixed.

5. From readings to a school year

A learner has up to six results a year: the GST, BOSY, the three MOSYs and EOSY. BASA follows the reading coordinator's rules for combining them:

  • Comprehension carries up. A round's comprehension is the higher of its own score and the best so far, starting from the GST. A bad day never demotes a learner.
  • Word reading carries up too. Accuracy and speed are each the best so far, so a reader never falls back to Non-Reader. Only oral readings count for accuracy; a silent reading's 100% is used only while there is no oral reading.
  • A round not given keeps the previous round's result.
  • Two values per round:
    • carried: the best so far, used by default in reports and ARAL;
    • actual: only that round's own reading, for planning.
    Each school chooses, per list, which one it uses: ARAL, BOSY masterlist, Form 2b, SRP, learner pages, ranking. Tutors never see this choice; their pages show plain results.
  • Status of each round:
    • assessed: the round's own reading has words, time and a comprehension entry;
    • carried over: a result exists only from an earlier round or the GST;
    • not assessed: no result at all.
    Progress monitoring, reminders and the dashboard count only assessed rounds.
  • No reading all year (no GST, no reading): reports and ARAL count the learner as Struggling, 3 levels down. This is a stand-in that any real result replaces.
  • Blank comprehension takes the carried % if there is one; otherwise it counts as 0, but stays marked as blank (different from a real 0).
  • Foundational Literacy Test (a reading without questions): a school setting decides whether such a round counts as assessed.
  • End of year: the EOSY result if EOSY was given; otherwise the best round given (a later round wins a tie).
  • Retakes: only the latest attempt of a round counts.

8. Defending the design: likely questions

Why does BASA use one passage of the learner's own grade instead of the manual's up-and-down passage ladder?
Without BASA: the manual has the teacher move to easier or harder passages until the learner's level is found. This takes several readings per learner and is rarely done fully in practice.
In BASA: the reading coordinator's practice is one grade-level passage per round. The adjusted level is then worked out from comprehension (section 4, step 3). It is faster, the same for every learner, and matches how the school actually assesses.
Can a list show the best result so far instead of each round's own reading?
Without BASA: whichever way a report is computed, nothing on it says which way it was.
In BASA: each round's own reading (actual) is always kept. A school can choose, list by list, to report the best result so far (carried), in case its division asks for that; every exported list states which values it used, and the ranking of the learners who need help most uses each round's own reading.
Why is a learner with no assessment counted as Struggling instead of being left out?
Without BASA: an unassessed learner is simply absent from the counts, so a section that tests fewer learners can look better.
In BASA: every learner must have a GST and a reading. Until they do, they count as Struggling (3 levels down), which keeps the counts honest and puts them on the ARAL and reminder lists. Monitoring screens label them not assessed, and any real result replaces the stand-in at once.
Why can a learner never read the same passage twice, even in another year?
Answer: a learner who has read a passage remembers it, so the second reading measures memory, not reading. BASA compares the passage text, not its id, because the same story appears in the 2018 set, the 2024 set and the graded-passage booklet. When a learner runs out of passages, the coordinator adds more.
Why are the bands adjustable but Table 8 fixed?
Answer: the acceptable ranges change. The coordinator's older workbook put Non-Readers at 50% and below; the DepEd bands BASA now uses put them at 35% and below. So the numbers are settings. Table 8 is the method of combining the measures, not a number. Changing it would be a different instrument.
Why round to whole percents?
Answer: the published bands are whole numbers (e.g. 90–96%). Without rounding, 96.5% would fall between bands. BASA rounds half up, as the workbook does.
Why does the NLA report use different bands from the rest of BASA?
Answer: the NLA portal asks for its own categories (GLR, 2-LD and 3-LD, each with Independent, Instructional and Frustration) and its own conversion table. The coordinator must be able to copy BASA's numbers straight into the portal, so that report follows the portal. Everything else follows the Phil-IRI tables.
Why finalize a round, and why store the cut-offs then?
Without BASA: a submitted report and the workbook can drift apart once someone edits the workbook.
In BASA: finalizing freezes the official results of a section's round. Storing the cut-offs means a later threshold change can't alter what was reported. Results can still be recomputed with today's cut-offs for comparison, without overwriting the stored ones.
Why does word reading use only oral readings?
Answer: a silent reading has no miscues, so its "100%" isn't a measured accuracy. It supplies speed, and counts for word reading only while the learner has no oral reading at all. The ranking lists such learners separately.
Why may any grade-level passage be used in any round?
Answer: the reading coordinator's rule: the pretest, MOSY and posttest labels in the booklets are a suggestion, and the real constraint is "never the same passage twice". BASA lists the round's labelled passages first.

9. Limits and known gaps

  • Results are recomputed from the raw readings with the current rules; only the threshold numbers are stored. If the rules (not just the numbers) change after release, old results would shift. This must be handled before any such change.
  • English only; Filipino Phil-IRI is out of scope.
  • Some printed answer keys looked wrong and were kept as printed, with a flag in the seed files.
